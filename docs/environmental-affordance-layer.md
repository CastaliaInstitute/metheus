# Environmental affordances and designed “channels”

**Status:** proposed Metheus extension, version 0.1. This describes optional ways to shape a person's environment around a chosen need or activity. It does not change a Promethean chart, validate Human Design, or prescribe a health, developmental, or relationship intervention.

## Three meanings to keep apart

1. **Chart definition** is a calculated Promethean property under a named Human Design source and calculation. A Metheus reading must not recalculate it from a conversation, a wearable, a life event, or an AI interaction.
2. **Conditioning or temporary completion** is language used within Human Design lineages for what may happen when another person or a transit supplies a complementary gate. It remains a statement within that symbolic system. For example, the [Jovian Archive glossary](https://jovianarchive.com/pages/human-design-dictionary) describes a channel as formed by the joining of two activated gates and says a dormant gate in an undefined center may be affected by another or a transit.
3. **A Metheus environmental affordance** is a real, revisable support someone may choose to make available around a goal or need: a routine, space, object, prompt, practice, peer, group, professional, or digital tool. It can offer a *functional analogue* to a theme the person wants to explore. It does not literally define a gate, complete a channel, or turn an undefined center into a deficit.

Metheus can use an undefined gate or channel as an optional **question prompt**. In a chart-aware mode the person explicitly enables, a source-versioned calculated map or a clearly identified user-supplied gate list can provide candidate complementary-gate activities. That chart match is a symbolic personalization cue, not evidence of a need, gap, benefit, or lack of ability. The AI may offer at most one candidate when it clearly fits the person's stated goal; the person can accept, revise, decline, or ignore it. If they want help with a practical task or need, the support should also be grounded in their stated goal, task, access context, developmental context, or other lived information.

## A channel theme supported by the environment

People sometimes describe a relationship or a transit as temporarily completing a hanging gate in Human Design. Metheus can represent that as a source-attributed **symbolic account**. It can also help someone design a repeatable context that supports a function they associate with that theme. Call this **environmentally supported expression** or a **functional channel analogue**—not synthetic chart activation.

| What is present | Metheus wording | What it does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person with a complementary gate, in Human Design terminology | “This relationship is described in this source as temporarily completing the channel.” | A proven biological mechanism or a permanent change to either chart. |
| A room, routine, tool, group, or AI scaffold chosen for a practical purpose | “You chose this context to make [function] easier to practice.” | That the environment has a gate, changes the chart, or guarantees the same experience as a relationship. |
| A user's report after trying the context | “You reported [experience] after this trial.” | Proof that the symbolic mapping caused the experience or that it will generalize. |

The useful design unit is intentionally simple: **theme → chosen function → support → your report**. For instance, a person may connect a chart theme with expressing ideas, choose the practical goal “practice speaking up in meetings,” and try a weekly low-stakes rehearsal with a peer, a private voice-note prompt, or an AI role-play. The practice context may help them enact the chosen function. Metheus records which option they selected and what they later report; it does not call the channel activated.

### Chart-aware complementary prompts

With explicit chart-aware mode enabled, Metheus can use the active-gate list from a person's selected calculated map, or from a clearly labeled self-supplied list, to identify candidate complementary-gate activities from the [AI affordance catalog](../catalog/gate-ai-affordances.json). Candidate matching is a simple lookup over the named, versioned BodyGraph topology:

1. Start from a gate marked active in the subject's authorized, calculated chart map.
2. Find its channel edge in that map's named topology version.
3. If the partner gate is not active in that same map, offer the partner gate's AI activity as an optional symbolic exploration.
4. If there are several possible partner gates, provide them as candidates without ranking them by chart alone. The AI may mention at most one when it clearly supports the person's stated goal; the person can accept, change, or dismiss the activity.
5. If both gates are active, describe the channel as defined only within the named Human Design calculation. An AI activity can still explore its theme, but it is not a “missing-gate” complement.

The chart supplies the **candidate prompt**, not a claim that the person lacks the partner function or needs it supplied. Do not say the AI has activated, completed, or defined a gate/channel. Keep natal chart activations separate from time-bounded transits; a transit must never silently overwrite or be merged into the person's natal chart. If the calculated map is partial, uncertain, or unavailable, do not infer a complementary gate—offer the full catalog as user-browsable ideas instead.

Both chart-aware paths produce the shared [`chart-aware AI context`](../schema/chart-aware-ai-context.schema.json). Its machine-readable `chartProvenance.chartBinding` distinguishes a map selected for the reading subject from a sender-self-attested but unverified gate list. For a selected calculated map, the payload includes the stated engine and version, calculation method and version, execution mode, time-zone database source/version, input precision, and an explicit statement that Metheus has not independently verified the calculation. These provenance fields characterize the supplied map; they do not establish engine trustworthiness. The authorized local selector also checks map selection, subject match, schema, and consent; the Matrix bot requires the `my-gates:` command prefix after room-wide opt-in but cannot authenticate ownership. Either context carries all active gate titles, complete channels, and the complete, unranked set of complementary activity candidates. The model may offer one candidate when it fits the stated goal; no candidate is prioritized by chart alone. Neither context includes birth date, time, location, subject ID, or map record ID. Both carry guidance to use symbolism only when relevant, avoid biology or capacity inference, and provide equivalent help without chart framing. The schema standardizes model-payload provenance; it does not authenticate chart ownership, independently verify a calculation, decide which provider receives the payload, or enforce runtime consent. Remote use still requires the separate remote-processing check.

The [calculated-natal context example](../examples/chart-aware-ai-context-calculated-natal.example.json) demonstrates only the context shape. It uses placeholder engine/time-zone values, explicitly says no calculation occurred, and is not a chart that the selector or AI should treat as real.

The model should receive only the minimum derived symbolic context needed for a requested or enabled chart-aware interaction, such as gate numbers and the named map/topology version. Do not send raw birth date, time, or location to an AI when the derived gate list is sufficient. In a shared couple/family room, never expose a member's chart or use it for a room answer without that chart owner's explicit choice; make clear that the response will be visible to the room and which selected chart data will go to any remote model. A private interaction is the default for personal chart exploration.

**Example:** a person's selected chart shows Gate 11 active and Gate 56 inactive in channel 11–56. Metheus can offer: “Your selected chart maps Gate 11 to the 11–56 channel and leaves Gate 56 as its unactivated partner. Want to try a short, Gate 56-inspired story or curiosity prompt? This is an optional symbolic exercise; it does not change your chart.” The useful, plain-language activity remains available even if the person does not know or accept the gate interpretation.

Supports can be deliberately composed. A “designed channel context” might combine a predictable time, a physical cue, a prompt, a sensory setting, and a tool or willing human participant. Each component should serve a stated function, be optional, and be adjustable. The design should not imply that a person is incomplete, that a relationship is needed to supply a missing trait, or that an AI can replace reciprocal human connection. In family or child contexts, caregiver participation and developmentally appropriate human support remain central.

For clarity in the interface, show the selected function in ordinary language first. Keep the gate/channel correspondence in a secondary “symbolic lens” detail that can be hidden. A compact card could read:

> **What you want to make easier:** speaking up in meetings<br>
> **Context you chose:** a 5-minute rehearsal before the meeting<br>
> **Symbolic lens (optional):** you associated this with your selected channel theme<br>
> **Afterward:** record whether it felt useful, neutral, difficult, or something else

This language makes the model legible to people who do not know Human Design and avoids treating symbolism as a measurement.

### Worked example: AI as a curiosity prompt for 11–56

Human Design source material names 11–56 the **Channel of Curiosity**; in the bodygraph topology used here, it connects Ajna and Throat. Metheus can let a user choose that as a symbolic lens, then offer an AI activity built around their stated interest: ask an open question, surface an unexpected but relevant idea, invite the person to make a connection, and offer a choice to explore further, tell a story, or stop. The AI is creating a context for curiosity and expression. In the user's metaphor, it may “stimulate” the channel; in Metheus records, it is an **AI-supported curiosity practice**, not gate activation or chart definition.

This also keeps the neuropsychology legible. Curiosity is a subjective and context-sensitive state, not a single hormone or a biomarker that an AI can measure. Experimental work has linked reported curiosity with reward and memory-related brain activity, including a dopaminergic circuit; that does not show that a particular prompt reliably raises dopamine, nor that the 11–56 symbolism explains a person's response. Metheus should record the prompt activity and the person's own report (for example, “interested,” “overloaded,” or “not engaging”), not infer a hormone level from chat behavior.

**Example interface card**

> **Explore an idea**<br>
> Your goal: follow a question about deep-sea life.<br>
> Try: ask Metheus for one surprising connection, then choose whether to hear a short explanation or tell your own version.<br>
> Optional symbolic lens: you associated this activity with Human Design channel 11–56, traditionally called the Channel of Curiosity.<br>
> Afterward: note whether it felt engaging, neutral, tiring, or something else.

The activity should invite exploration without manufacturing urgency, endless novelty, or dependence on the chatbot. The user can choose topic, depth, pacing, and whether to continue. The 11–56 lens remains optional and must not affect the quality or availability of the activity.

## From prompt to optional environment design

1. **Keep the chart statement attributed.** Name the source and lineage; show its undefined/hanging-gate vocabulary as a chart description, not a clinical or psychological finding.
2. **Translate the theme into a function, not an identity.** Ask whether the person wants more opportunity for communication practice, reflection, learning, structure, connection, problem solving, or another self-chosen function.
3. **Ground design in lived context.** Use only selected evidence such as the person's stated goal, repeated report, task demands, access constraints, caregiver-supported developmental context, or feedback from previous attempts. Do not infer a missing capacity from an undefined chart feature.
4. **Offer alternatives.** A person might prefer a human peer, group, physical cue, routine, professional support, AI conversation, or no additional support. Present costs, privacy, availability, and limits in plain language when relevant.
5. **Let the person decide whether to try it.** A reflection option is an invitation, not a recommendation. Record proposed, chosen, declined, revised, and actually used separately.
6. **Ask what happened.** Record the person's later experience or task-specific observation separately from the affordance. A trial can feel useful, neutral, uncomfortable, inaccessible, or mixed. One response does not validate the symbolic pairing or establish a general effect.

The draft [reading schema](../schema/reading.schema.json) permits an `environmental_affordance` reflection option. It requires a non-symbolic basis and source record, and it records the delivery mode, intended function, and role frame. The [observation schema](../schema/observation.schema.json) can separately record an affordance lifecycle event (`offered`, `chosen`, `declined`, `unavailable`, `used`, `stopped`, or revised) and later observations can link to its stable `affordanceId`. The [synthetic AI-use example](../examples/environmental-affordance-ai-use.example.json) and its distinct [uncertain response](../examples/environmental-affordance-response.example.json) show this split; the [linked case bundle](../examples/environmental-affordance-linked-case.example.json) combines them with the originating reading and selected record IDs. The PWA's **Record trying this activity** composer exports that same pair of record types locally; gate/channel tags are optional and chart context is omitted by default. The in-memory [`validate_environmental_affordance_links`](../metheus/affordances.py) resolver checks that selected lifecycle events point to an existing same-subject reading option, preserve its delivery/function/role metadata, and that selected response records point to a selected same-subject affordance trial. [`summarize_authorized_affordance_trial`](../metheus/affordance_trials.py) evaluates local-analysis consent for the exact selected records before it calls [`summarize_affordance_trial`](../metheus/affordance_trials.py), which makes an ephemeral, source-preserving view of the chosen trial's lifecycle events, pairwise lifecycle timing, separately reported experience values, and event/report timing. The output contract is the [trial-summary schema](../schema/environmental-affordance-trial-summary.schema.json), illustrated by a [synthetic summary](../examples/affordance-trial-summary.example.json). The local [summary command](../tools/summarize_affordance_trial.py) accepts a PWA export and a valid consent grant, validates the records, and writes a new owner-only summary file or prints the summary to stdout; it does not upload records. For example: `python tools/summarize_affordance_trial.py --records trial-export.json --grants local-grants.json --purpose personal_reflection --output trial-summary.json`. The [synthetic trial consent example](../examples/affordance-trial-consent.example.json) authorizes local analysis only for the two synthetic trial records; it is not a real grant. To run the full synthetic flow, use `python tools/summarize_affordance_trial.py --records examples/environmental-affordance-linked-case.example.json --grants examples/affordance-trial-consent.example.json --purpose learning_support --output /tmp/metheus-affordance-summary.json`. The grant must authorize analysis of every exported record for the named subject and purpose. [`render_affordance_trial_summary_html`](../metheus/rendering.py) displays events, reports, timing relations, source IDs, and limitations as distinct sections in an escaped standalone page. Regenerate the [synthetic HTML example](../examples/affordance-trial-summary.html) with `python tools/render_affordance_trial_summary.py examples/affordance-trial-summary.example.json examples/affordance-trial-summary.html`. It includes only explicitly selected current records, keeps events and reports separate, and makes no effectiveness or causal claim; lifecycle and report relations describe recorded timing only and do not impose an expected sequence. It does not store a history or enforce storage access. Sending this result to a remote model would require a separate remote-processing authorization for the same inputs.

For example, a caller can request the synthetic use and response records with `summarize_authorized_affordance_trial(records, grants, subject_id="example-subject-01", affordance_id="synthetic-ai-dialogue-practice-01", purpose="learning_support", selected_record_ids=["synthetic-ai-affordance-use-01", "synthetic-ai-affordance-response-01"])`. The wrapper first evaluates each selected record against the supplied current consent grants for local analysis. The result retains the recorded `used` event and the separate `uncertain` report with their source IDs and timestamps; the temporal relation describes only whether those recorded windows precede, follow, or overlap. It does not authorize sending the summary to an AI provider.

The [`select_authorized_complementary_gate_affordances`](../metheus/affordances.py) wrapper checks the selected same-subject map and symbolic-analysis consent, checks remote-processing permission when requested, selects unranked complement ideas, and emits a validated [chart-aware AI context](../schema/chart-aware-ai-context.schema.json) containing all active gate numbers and complete channels. The underlying [`select_complementary_gate_affordances`](../metheus/affordances.py) remains a pure symbolic lookup and does not authorize access. Reading preflight runs the lifecycle-link check before consented analysis. `assemble_reading` carries reflection options into the output package with selected sources and subject status, and the HTML presenter renders them as optional ideas rather than recommendations; the [synthetic assembled option](../examples/assembled-reading-ai-affordance.example.json) demonstrates the presentation contract, and the [rendered HTML example](../examples/assembled-reading-ai-affordance.html) shows the actual presentation. Regenerate it with `python tools/render_reading_example.py examples/assembled-reading-ai-affordance.example.json examples/assembled-reading-ai-affordance.html`. The link validator does not validate event ordering, require every lifecycle step, interpret AI disclosure quality, assess effectiveness or safety, or infer causality; those remain separate decisions and evidence questions.

Consent remains tied to the actual record's domain and purpose. For example, an AI dialogue used for a learning goal is a learning record; if the subject separately reports experiencing it as relationship-like, that account belongs under the relationship domain and sensitive privacy scope. Analysis involving a remote AI service requires the separate remote-processing permission already defined in the [privacy model](privacy-consent-model.md). Do not store prompts or transcripts as part of the affordance event; an explicit, separate purpose and data-retention decision would be required for any such content.

A synthetic follow-up-reading test now exercises this chain: the original reading offers an AI dialogue scaffold, the subject separately reports trying it, and a later record says the experience felt uncertain. The follow-up reading selects and independently authorizes the use event and response, cites them in separate observation claims, and includes a boundary that neither proves benefit nor harm. Withdrawing either record's consent makes preflight fail. This is still an in-memory fixture: it does not store actual AI conversations, authorize a storage service, or evaluate whether the scaffold works.

The calculated-map selector remains an in-memory prototype; it does not authenticate a chart owner or enforce a storage boundary. The Matrix bot prototype accepts a sender-supplied active-gate list for a single `ask-chart` request or through an explicit per-sender `chart-set` room setting. The latter remains in process memory only and can be cleared by the sender; opt-out, membership changes, or restart clear it. Both paths derive complete channels and optional complementary activities from local catalogs. The bot does not calculate or verify charts, create a persistent personal chart store, or rank or evaluate supports. It is not deployed, and the PWA has no participant chart profile or chart calculator.

## AI-mediated scaffold

An AI conversation could be one designed affordance. It might offer a repeatable place to rehearse a conversation, organize thoughts, explore alternatives, or practice a skill when a person chooses that function. A user may personally describe the interaction as relationship-like; Metheus can preserve that as the user's account. The system should still describe the service's role as a **tool or scaffold**, not claim that the AI has feelings, reciprocal attachment, human judgment, or a Human Design gate/channel.

An AI scaffold should:

- disclose that the partner is AI, what it is intended to do, and what it cannot do;
- keep the subject in control of the topic, memory, privacy choices, and ability to stop or delete the interaction;
- avoid exclusivity, emotional-dependency cues, pressure to return, claims of love or need, and framing itself as a replacement for friends, caregivers, clinicians, or community;
- keep private or intimate details optional and minimize storage and third-party information;
- connect practice to the person's chosen real-world activity when desired, without demanding transfer to human relationships;
- offer alternatives such as journaling, independent rehearsal, a trusted person, an accessibility tool, or no interaction;
- keep the response and its perceived after-effect as separate records; do not label an AI output as evidence, therapy, diagnosis, or a biological signal.

For babies and young children, an AI scaffold must not be represented as an attachment figure, caregiver, or substitute for responsive human care. Any age-appropriate use should support caregiver-child interaction or a concrete learning/access need, with appropriate stewardship and review; Metheus currently has no child-facing AI service.

## Example

A person has an undefined channel in a chart and asks whether it explains why they find language practice easier with a conversation partner. Metheus may state the chart's source-attributed description, then keep it separate from the person's own account that short, low-pressure French practice helps them prepare. It could ask whether they want to try a human peer, an AI tool that role-plays a dialogue, solo rehearsal, or nothing. If they choose the AI practice, the option is grounded in their stated learning goal and prior feedback, not in the chart. Later feedback might say it felt useful or uncomfortable; neither result changes their chart definition or proves that the symbolic theme caused the experience.

## Model boundary

The Promethean map may select a candidate symbolic prompt only when the person enables chart-aware mode. It must not establish a need, deficit, capacity, or whether an experiment succeeded. An environmental affordance belongs to context; a person's decision to use it belongs to behavior; their response belongs to a separately sourced observation. If Metheus later compares repeated trials, it must describe within-person timing and uncertainty and avoid causal language unless a suitable reviewed design supports it.
