# Human Design × Neuroendocrine Atlas

**Atlas framework v1.0 — comparative systems model**
**Status:** hypothesis and reflection framework; not a validated biological map.

This atlas places Human Design's nine-center, 64-gate, 36-channel bodygraph beside neuroendocrine physiology as two different kinds of model. It asks whether the symbolic vocabulary can help people formulate useful questions about lived experience. It does not claim that centers are glands, gates regulate hormones, channels are biological pathways, or chart features predict an individual's physiology. No Human Design–endocrine association has been established scientifically.

## Evidence labels

Keep three kinds of comparison separate:

| Relation | Meaning | Example | Not evidence of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anatomical analogy | A symbolic center is discussed alongside a body region or organ | Throat and thyroid | Shared structure or function |
| Functional analogy | A symbolic theme resembles a broad physiological topic | Solar Plexus themes and visceral signaling | A specific hormone mechanism |
| Network analogy | A symbolic channel is compared with interaction between regulatory systems | A channel and a feedback loop | A physical connection or direction of causation |

Each claim should also carry an evidence status: `source_attributed_symbolism`, `reflective_analogy`, `general_biomedical_mechanism`, or `empirically_tested_association`. An established biological mechanism does not validate a symbolic association. These labels extend the safeguards in the [Promethean–Epimethean bridge](promethean-epimethean-bridge.md) and [evidence-claim model](evidence-claim-model.md).

## Nine centers: candidate conversation domains

These are possible topics to place side by side in a reading, not anatomical assignments. The bodygraph's “Solar Plexus,” for example, is a symbolic center name and should not be mistaken for the anatomical celiac plexus. The hypothalamus and pituitary coordinate multiple axes; neither can be assigned exclusively to Head or Ajna. Likewise, many hormones and neurotransmitters act across tissues and networks.

| Center (gate count) | Symbolic theme (broad) | Possible biological conversation domain | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head (3) | Inspiration, questions, mental pressure | Circadian inputs, hypothalamic regulation, cortical attention | Not the hypothalamus or pineal gland |
| Ajna (6) | Concepts, interpretation, certainty | Distributed cortical cognition and predictive processing | Not the pituitary; cognition is not one endocrine output |
| Throat (11) | Expression, communication, manifestation | Speech and motor systems; thyroid physiology may be discussed as separate context | Thyroid hormones are regulated through a distributed HPT feedback axis |
| G / Identity (8) | Identity, direction, love | Distributed self-representation, social cognition, attachment research | Oxytocin and dopamine are not “identity hormones” |
| Heart / Ego / Will (4) | Will, value, promises, resources | Circulatory and effort/recovery context; cardiac natriuretic peptides regulate volume and pressure | ANP/BNP do not establish a will or resource center |
| Solar Plexus (7) | Emotion, sensitivity, emotional change | Emotion-related neural/autonomic circuits, gut–brain signaling, pancreatic and intestinal metabolic signals | Not an anatomical plexus or a single emotional organ |
| Sacral (9) | Response, energy, reproductive themes | Activity and reproductive context; hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis | Gonadal hormones are not a measure of “sacral definition” |
| Spleen (7) | Instinct, immediacy, wellbeing | Interoception, autonomic responses, immune signaling | Immune function is distributed; cytokines and catecholamines are not spleen-specific hormones |
| Root (9) | Pressure, stress, drive | HPA axis, sympathetic-adrenal-medullary (SAM) response, allostatic adaptation | Cortisol, catecholamines and aldosterone have different control systems and time courses |

The parenthetical counts sum to 64 gates. They identify the traditional BodyGraph partition only; they do not imply 64 physiological units.

These refinements matter: the HPT axis includes hypothalamic TRH, pituitary TSH, thyroid T4/T3, and feedback; GLP-1 is produced principally by intestinal enteroendocrine L cells and acts on pancreatic and other targets; cortisol from the adrenal cortex and catecholamines from the adrenal medulla are related to stress but are not the same signal or pathway; aldosterone is principally regulated through renin–angiotensin and potassium rather than being a generic stress output. [Endotext: HPT-axis physiology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK278958/), [Endotext: hypothalamus and pituitary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279126/), [Endotext: gut hormones and metabolism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK556470/), [NCBI Bookshelf: adrenal gland physiology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537260/), [Endotext: natriuretic peptides](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279096/).

## Awareness centers as a comparison prompt

The traditional Human Design grouping of Ajna (cognitive), Spleen (instinctive), and Solar Plexus (emotional) awareness can be a useful reflective contrast. Neurobiology does not isolate these into three independent awareness organs: cognition, interoception, emotion, autonomic regulation, memory, and immune/endocrine signaling interact across distributed circuits. Metheus may ask what a person noticed, thought, and felt, while treating those as separate self-reports rather than inferred hormone states.

## Gate-level annotations

The machine-readable [BodyGraph topology catalog](../catalog/human-design-bodygraph-topology.json) now covers all 64 source-specific gate titles, their nine-center membership, and the 36 symbolic channel connections. Contract validation compares its center and channel assignments with the PWA's adapted generic SVG. Gate titles are lineage-specific labels, not biological constructs. The catalog deliberately assigns no gate a hormone, gland, neurochemical, or physiological mechanism, and the channel graph is not yet suitable as a chart-calculation source.

## Network architecture: map actual biology first

The more defensible comparison is between two graph representations, with different node and edge types:

1. Represent the Promethean bodygraph as a versioned symbolic graph: centers, gates, and channels.
2. Represent biological systems from cited physiology sources: organs/tissues, signals, receptors, feedback relations, time scales, and contexts.
3. Keep both graphs intact. Add a third, explicitly authored analogy layer only when a source, purpose, and uncertainty statement are supplied.
4. Never translate a symbolic edge into a biological edge, or combine the graphs into one causal network.

For example, HPT, HPA, HPG, circadian, gut–brain, immune-endocrine, and autonomic networks can be described on their own terms and then placed beside symbolic features as optional prompts. Endocrine networks involve feedback and distributed regulation; resemblance between graph shapes is not evidence of biological equivalence.

Metheus now has an initial, source-attributed [candidate endocrine-relations graph](../catalog/endocrine-relations.json) for selected HPT, HPA, HPG, circadian–melatonin, glucose-regulation, and gut-incretin relations. Its edges remain under review, internal only, and ineligible for readings. This is a starting set for network analysis, not an exhaustive pathway map or individual model.

## Research questions and validation

One possible research question is whether prespecified chart features are associated with within-person variability in a named, validly measured outcome. This would test a statistical association, not a biological mechanism or individual diagnostic value.

Before data collection, specify:

- the chart calculation source, version, birth-data precision, and definition rules;
- a narrow hypothesis, primary outcome, sampling schedule, assay/device method, and analysis plan;
- a sample-size rationale, inclusion criteria, and independently justified comparison groups;
- relevant covariates such as age, sex-related physiology where appropriate, medication, sleep, shift work, season, latitude, circadian collection time, reproductive stage, and clinical status;
- blinded analysis, correction for multiple testing, missing-data handling, preregistration, replication, and external validation;
- privacy, consent, retention, deletion, and prohibition on diagnosis or consequential individual decisions.

Compare chart-based prediction against preregistered baselines, including suitable arbitrary or shuffled assignments and non-chart predictors. Do not search all centers/gates/channels against many biomarkers and report only the attractive matches. A positive association would need replication and could still reflect confounding, measurement artifacts, or chance.

## Reading language

Prefer: “This Human Design source describes the center through an emotional theme. Your diary records mood and sleep on these dates; the timing overlaps in this sample. The records do not show that the chart feature or a hormone caused the change. Does this symbolic framing help you reflect on it?”

Avoid: “Your center controls this gland,” “this channel activates the hormone,” or “your chart predicts your stress response.”

This model is for reflection and research design. It is not a medical test, a hormone estimate, or a basis for changing treatment. Use laboratory values and clinical interpretation only with their actual source, collection conditions, and appropriate professional context.
